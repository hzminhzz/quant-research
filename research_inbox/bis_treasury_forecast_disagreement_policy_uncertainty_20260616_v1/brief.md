# bis_treasury_forecast_disagreement_policy_uncertainty_20260616_v1

## SOURCE_FACT
[BIS Working Paper 1361, 16 June 2026](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1361-bond-yield-responses-macro-news-role-macro-forecast-disagreement-and-monetary-policy-uncertainty), by Peter Hordahl, Burcin Kisacikoglu and Dora Xia. Examines intraday US Treasury futures and yield reactions to six US macro release categories from 1998-2024.

## AUTHOR_REPORTED_UNVERIFIED
Higher **pre-release forecast disagreement** is associated with **smaller** yield responses to a given macro surprise; higher **policy-rate uncertainty** is associated with **larger** responses. Inflation surprise sensitivity strengthened after COVID; payroll/jobs sensitivity conditional on uncertainty weakened. The report does not provide profitable trade rules or a net strategy Sharpe.

## LOCAL_INFERENCE
Conditional news-price sensitivity might be an economically distinctive event-volatility/risk-state hypothesis, **not** automatic directional trading alpha.

## ADAPTATION_PROPOSAL
A future separately approved test could benchmark a PIT-conditioned event response against surprise-only and regime-matched baselines. No model coefficients, orders, or data availability are invented here.

## UNKNOWN / ADVERSE
Precise source regressions, contracts, tickers, consensus vintage, future-price sign, release/feed latency, event-time quotes, costs and genuinely tradable reaction horizon. This is **THESIS_CAPTURED**, not an ATS executable candidate.
