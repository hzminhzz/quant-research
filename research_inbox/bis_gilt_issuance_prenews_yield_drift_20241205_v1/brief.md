# bis_gilt_issuance_prenews_yield_drift_20241205_v1 — Gilt supply and pre-news risk

## SOURCE_FACT
[BIS WP1232 (December 5, 2024)](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1232-bond-supply-yield-drifts-and-liquidity-provision-macroeconomic-announcements), and [Bank of England SWP998 (2022; revised October 2024)](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2022/bond-supply-price-drifts-and-liquidity-provision-before-central-bank-announcements), Dong Lou, Gábor Pintér, Semih Üslü and Danny Walker. **UK cash gilts** yield increases during **the two days before scheduled BoE policy or labour-market releases**, especially around new issuance. This is not the US Treasury month-end premium.

## AUTHOR_REPORTED_UNVERIFIED
The authors report higher term premium, dealer inventory avoidance and a change from hedge-fund to more passive liquidity providers during issuance-linked pre-news periods. Their transaction-level evidence was not reproduced.

## LOCAL_INFERENCE
Inventory constraints and information risk jointly exert downward cash-bond price pressure (yield up implies price down at stable duration). It is not yet evidence that a retail short-gilt trade is profitable.

## UNKNOWN / ADVERSE
Exact maturity/issue definition, order time, implied interest rate expectation decomposition, spread, financing and post-news risk. Announcement surprises and monetary regime may dominate conditional effects. Source does not specify stops, entry print, exits, tradable notional or net returns.
