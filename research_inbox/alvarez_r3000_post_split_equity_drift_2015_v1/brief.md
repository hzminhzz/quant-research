# alvarez_r3000_post_split_equity_drift_2015_v1

**Status:** THESIS_CAPTURED — descriptive research only; no ATS scientific or execution approval.

## SOURCE_FACT
Cesar Alvarez, *Trading stock splits* (2015-01-28), https://alvarezquanttrading.com/blog/trading-stock-splits/ — sections "Finding a stock split", "Test parameters", "Before the split. After the split", "N-Month hold test" and "Profit Stops/Stop Losses". The author inferred effective dates for 2:1–10:1 stock splits from adjusted versus raw prices, checked 20 against another site, and tested historically constituted Russell 3000 equities (2005–2014).

The **post-split** variant considers entries within the first two weeks after the effective date, buys at the next open with a 21-session Close*Volume average above $1m, up to ten positions of 10% each. Author-tested exit holds: 1, 3, 6, 12 months. Separate stop/profit variants: closing loss at least 10%, closing gain at least 50% or 100%, or time expiry, exits next open. These are not a single frozen combination.

## AUTHOR_REPORTED_UNVERIFIED
The author reports selected periods outperformed SPY at similar risk, especially 12-month holds, and acknowledged drawdowns were still high. His 500-run random-entry check is not independent reproduction. The pre-split entry branch presumed two weeks of public knowledge without historical verification.

## LOCAL_INFERENCE
Corporate-action publicity may proxy attention and past winners. A pure stock-split causal premium has **not** been established; prior momentum could explain the results. Portfolio risk driver: long US equity, corporate action/attention, multi-month holding, high likely overlap with equity momentum. Correlations not measured.

## ADAPTATION_PROPOSAL
No instrument substitution or execution implementation. A future study could compare split stocks against momentum-, size-, sector- and liquidity-matched non-split controls, preserving event-date knowledge and corporate actions.

## UNKNOWN / ADVERSE EVIDENCE
PIT effective-date provider, announcement availability, stock delisting, dividend adjustment, exact calendar-month handling, broker fees and opening slippage. Extreme winner/survivorship bias and long hold exposure are material. Source-reported performance is not after-cost evidence. No market data queried in this capture.
