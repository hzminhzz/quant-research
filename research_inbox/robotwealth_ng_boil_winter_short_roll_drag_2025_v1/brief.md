# Natural gas winter seasonality, roll-down and leveraged ETF short exposure

**Primary source:** Kris Longmore, *Lessons from a Volatile Seasonality Trade*, 20 December 2025. https://edgealchemy.robotwealth.com/p/lessons-from-a-volatile-seasonality

## SOURCE_FACT
- Longmore describes shorting natural-gas futures around winter months, and says he prefers to express it as short BOIL, a leveraged long natural-gas ETF. These products differ and must not be silently substituted.
- Source attributes opportunity to winter seasonality, futures-curve roll-down and leveraged ETF volatility drag.
- Source says the trade is held for roughly four months annually; exact entry/exit dates are not published in this article.
- Author describes maintaining roughly constant dollar short exposure through rebalancing after material drift, without an exact quantitative trigger or interval.
- Source location: introductory paragraphs and "This Year's Ride" in article, dated 20 December 2025.

## AUTHOR_REPORTED_UNVERIFIED
- Article cites about 12% CAGR, 0.5 Sharpe and 50% annualized volatility, in the market four months a year. These are author claims, not reproduced estimates; fee, borrow and sample conventions are incompletely stated.
- Author's 2025 illustration reports an un-rebalanced short growing roughly 35% above intended exposure amid drawdown.

## LOCAL_INFERENCE — Not source-proven alpha
Potential returns may combine compensated winter weather risk, curve carry and ETF path dependence. Null: any advantage vanishes once actual short borrow, futures term structure, margin and loss constraints are included.

## ADVERSE EVIDENCE AND RISK
- Winter cold snaps or supply shocks can create sharp price rallies and losses for a short natural gas position.
- Leveraged ETF shorting adds recall/borrow risk, margin and path dependence. Exact borrow fees and ability to short are unknown.
- The historical source numbers do not establish standalone after-cost profitability or correlation with other strategies.
