# perp-dex-tools source review for metals dual grid — 2026-10-10

**Status: CONCEPTUAL REFERENCE ONLY. Not a strategy validation or deployment recommendation.**

Reviewed the default-branch source of [`your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools`](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools) (not a pinned commit). Source paths and line links below refer to `main` as inspected on 2026-10-10.

## Source facts

- `trading_bot.py` models one configured `ticker`, one `direction`, fixed `quantity`, a take-profit percentage, `max_orders`, and timing/grid/stop parameters; the close side is the opposite direction ([config and side](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/trading_bot.py#L20-L49)). It is a repeated open-limit → opposing close-limit loop, not a price-level grid with 20 independently managed rungs ([open handling](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/trading_bot.py#L165-L254)). A close target derives from that order's reported entry price and configured take-profit percentage; it is not a per-lot cashflow ledger targeting return-to-entry after costs ([close calculation](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/trading_bot.py#L210-L245)).
- `grid_step` constrains spacing between close orders in the bot loop; it does not express fixed-anchor entry levels or 25-bps adverse increments ([grid handling](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/trading_bot.py#L340-L435)). Its controls are a maximum count of active close orders, wait-time adjustment, and price stop/pause behavior—not a $200 marked drawdown limit or emergency-loss exit ([control loop](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/trading_bot.py#L340-L520)).
- The order callback recognizes `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, but the bot's open-fill path responds to full `FILLED`; on canceled opens it records filled amount, while the close order is submitted for configured full quantity rather than a demonstrated per-lot residual reconciliation ([callback](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/trading_bot.py#L90-L157), [result handling](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/trading_bot.py#L205-L310)). This is not evidence of robust partial-fill accounting for the proposed lot lifecycle.
- Lighter market setup obtains a ticker's market ID and supported size/price decimal multipliers from Lighter order-book metadata, and derives tick size from book details ([market setup](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/exchanges/lighter.py#L35-L75), [contract attributes](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/exchanges/lighter.py#L490-L555)). Lighter limit orders use `ORDER_TYPE_LIMIT`, GTT, `reduce_only=False`; the source does not establish passive post-only behavior ([order params](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/exchanges/lighter.py#L250-L310)). New open price is derived from websocket BBO midpoint and adjusted around existing close orders, not from historical OHLCV price-touch fills ([price selection](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/exchanges/lighter.py#L350-L390)).
- Lighter's adapter keeps a single `current_order` state, waits up to 10 seconds for a fill in `place_open_order`, and returns that current order's fields; close submission waits five seconds then reports `OPEN` ([order lifecycle](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/exchanges/lighter.py#L310-L350)). Its active-order conversion includes a source `FIXME` that `size` is wrong, and assigns remaining size to `size` ([active orders](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/exchanges/lighter.py#L415-L475)). These implementation details are important limitations, not validated exchange behavior.
- `runbot.py` exposes one exchange/ticker/direction and scalar quantity/TP/order-count/time/grid/stop arguments; it creates one `TradingBot` ([CLI/config](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/runbot.py#L20-L110)). Its tests cover retry behavior, not grid accounting, Lighter partial fills, historical fills, or risk controls ([test](https://github.com/your-quantguy/perp-dex-tools/blob/main/tests/test_query_retry.py)).

## Fit to `metals_dual_grid_mvp`

**Reusable conceptually:** exchange-adapter separation and Lighter-specific discovery of market ID, tick and size precision are useful design precedents. Event-driven order updates and maintaining opposing exits after entry are also relevant concepts, subject to independent correctness work.

**Not supported or inferable from this project:** two simultaneously independent XAU-long / XAG-short controllers, fixed anchors, 20 entry levels at 25 bps, next-bar OHLCV price-touch plus volume-gate simulation, return-to-entry per-lot exits, independent rearming, beta-adjusted silver sizing, a $200 max-drawdown rule, or emergency exit cost assumptions. The live BBO/order submission loop supplies none of the historical bar/fill methodology or calibrated costs needed to assess those. A single directional bot and its max active close-order count are not equivalent to a two-market inventory/risk portfolio. Partial-fill callback awareness does not establish correct residual sizing, accounting, or safe exits.

## Context from existing local audits

The local Lighter audits document a separate historical simulation design rather than evidence from this repository: the anchored-volume note describes fixed anchors, independent cycles, external-volume gating, assumed costs, and historical funding limitations ([local note](lighter_anchored_volume_ab_20261009.md#L30-L93)); the earlier grid comparison warns that OHLCV path tests do not verify passive maker fills and omit key live execution risks ([local note](lighter_spy_qqq_grid_20261009.md#L40-L59)). Those caveats remain applicable; `perp-dex-tools` does not close the execution/data gap.
