Section 1: Matteo Conti's Institutional Prop Firm Engine
Matteo Conti (CIO at quantitative hedge fund SQR Capital and former market maker of 7 years) reverse-
engineered retail proprietary trading firm challenges. In challenge environments, traders face an asymmetric
constraint: reaching a profit target (e.g., +$3,000) before touching a trailing maximum drawdown (e.g., -$2,000)
within a bounded window.


  The Geometry Hypothesis: High reward-to-risk strategies (2:1 or 3:1) inherently have low win rates (~35–
  45%). In probability theory, low win-rate systems inevitably produce clusters of consecutive losses. In prop firm
  challenges, a 4–6 trade losing streak immediately breaches the trailing drawdown. Conti's suite intentionally
  uses lower reward-to-risk ratios (0.5:1 to 0.7:1) to engineer high win rates (65–70%), compressing losing
  streaks to prevent drawdown breaches.




1.1 Strategy 1: The "Volt Break" (Volatility Breakout)

A 30-minute momentum breakout designed to exploit the positive drift in equity index futures while strictly

   Daily ATR Benchmark: 15-period Simple Moving Average of the daily session ATR (SMA15(ATRDaily))

   Session Baseline ($P_{\text{open}}$): Open price of the session recorded at Midnight Central Time

        Noise Up Barrier = P_open + [ 0.30 × SMA_15(ATR_Daily) ]

   Volume Benchmark: Cumulative Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) anchored at Midnight Central

   Entry Trigger: A 30-minute bar must close above the Noise Up Barrier AND close above the session

   Execution Fill: Enter Long at the open of the next bar immediately following the qualifying bar close.

1.2 Strategy 2: VWAP Pullback with ADX Gate

A 1-minute microstructure strategy exploiting institutional algorithmic execution. Large funds execute
institutional orders benchmarked to VWAP. After an initial morning impulse, pullbacks to VWAP attract
aggressive institutional reloading.


Technical Specifications & Sequential State Machine
   Contract & Bar Timeframe: NASDAQ-100 Futures (NQ / MNQ), 1-minute bars, Long only.

   Opening Range (OR30): High and Low established between 8:30 AM CT and 9:00 AM CT (first 30 minutes
   of regular cash trading).

   VWAP: Intraday VWAP anchored at cash open or midnight.

   ADX Gate: Standard 14-period Average Directional Index (ADX).

The 4-Step Gating Sequence:

1. Gate 1 (Arming): Price breaks above the Opening Range High, and a 1-minute bar closes above the OR30
   High. This confirms morning institutional buying power.

2. Gate 2 (Pullback Touch): Price pulls back and touches the VWAP line (a low wick touching VWAP satisfies
   this; does not require closing below).

3. Gate 3 (Reclaim): A 1-minute bar closes back above the VWAP line. (Gates 2 and 3 can occur on the same
   candle via a wick to VWAP and a close above).

4. Gate 4 (Momentum Deceleration Gate): At the bar close above VWAP, ADX > 20 AND ADX is not
   increasing (ADXt ≤ ADXt-1, meaning flat or sloping downward). This proves the pullback's downward selling
   momentum has exhausted.


   Lagged ADX Edge Case: If Gates 1, 2, and 3 are satisfied but ADX is still increasing (downside trend
   intensifying), do NOT discard the setup. The algorithm pauses and waits for ADX to peak and print its first non-
   increasing bar (while price remains above VWAP and ADX > 20) before executing the entry.

1.3 Strategy 3: Overnight Bias Opening Range Breakout (ORB)

A 15-minute bi-directional breakout system leveraging global pre-market price discovery between 00:00 CT (or
23:00 CT) and 08:30 CT. Tailored for funded account phases to capture high reward-to-risk payouts (3:1 R:R).


Technical Specifications & 3-Tier Partitioning
   Contract & Timeframe: NASDAQ-100 Futures (NQ / MNQ), 15-minute bars, Long and Short.

   Overnight Discovery Range: High ($P_{\text{high}}$) and Low ($P_{\text{low}}$) recorded from Midnight
   CT (00:00 CT) to 8:30 AM CT.

   Range Calculation: $\text{Range} = P_{\text{high}} - P_{\text{low}}$.

   3-Tier Boundaries:
         Top 1/3 (Long Bias): $[P_{\text{low}} + 0.667 \times \text{Range}, \; P_{\text{high}}]$

         Middle 1/3 (Neutral - No Trade): $[P_{\text{low}} + 0.333 \times \text{Range}, \; P_{\text{low}} + 0.667
         \times \text{Range}]$

         Bottom 1/3 (Short Bias): $[P_{\text{low}}, \; P_{\text{low}} + 0.333 \times \text{Range}]$

   Session Opening Range (OR15): High and Low of the first 15-minute bar of regular cash trading (08:30 to
   08:45 CT).


Entry & Risk Mechanics
   Directional Bias at 08:30 CT:
         Cash Open in Top 1/3 → Long Bias Only.

         Cash Open in Bottom 1/3 → Short Bias Only.

         Cash Open in Middle 1/3 → NO TRADE for the session. (If open sits exactly on boundary, treat as
         neutral).

   Long Entry Trigger: Under Long Bias, a 15-minute bar closes above the 15-minute OR High AND ADX >
   20 (slope does not matter).

   Short Entry Trigger: Under Short Bias, a 15-minute bar closes below the 15-minute OR Low AND ADX >
   20.

   Intraday Retrace Edge Case: If Long Bias is established, price dips below the OR Low during morning
   trade, but subsequently rallies and closes above the OR High with ADX > 20, the trade is still valid.

Adopted from a former CIA operative mentor ("Mike"): entries represent only 20% of P&L; trade management

   The CCI +100 Rule: When the Commodity Channel Index ($\text{CCI}$) crosses above $+100$ following a
   long breakout entry, do NOT take profits at arbitrary targets. Hold the position until CCI crosses back below
   $+100$. This prolongs exposure to institutional tail events while cutting stagnation.




4.2 The Accelerating ATR Ratchet Trailing Stop

A dynamic trailing stop that tightens proportional to both volatility and time elapsed:

    Trailing Stop Level_t = Base Price + [ Multiplier × ATR × BarsInTrade ]

Starts 2 ATRs below entry. As bars accumulate, the stop accelerates upward to breakeven and locked profit,
protecting open equity against midday reversals.



4.3 Dynamic Position Sizing (DPS) via Performance Qualifiers

Win rates oscillate between 30% and 70% across market regimes. Instead of binary filters that overfit by
discarding trades, DPS modulates position size across 5 Qualifier Groups:

1. Market Contraction vs. Expansion cycles (Bollinger Band Width / ATR ratio).

2. Market Directional Strength (Higher-timeframe ADX and trend slope).

3. Volatility Regimes (Absolute and relative ATR percentiles).

4. Market Internals (Breadth, Advance/Decline, Volume Imbalance).

5. Time-of-day and Day-of-week Seasonality.

3-Tier Allocation Matrix:

   Low Probability Regime: Trade 0 contracts (skip trade) or 0.5 size.

   Average Probability Regime: Trade 1 contract (base size).

   High Probability Regime: Trade 2 contracts (double size).

Hedge Fund Result: Increased net profit by 178% while drawdown increased by only 29%, doubling the Profit-
to-Drawdown ratio from 4.3 to 9.3.

5.1.1 Hedge Fund Portfolio Construction & Friction Modeling
In hedge fund operations, building thousands of breakout strategies serves a single mathematical purpose:
brutal selectivity. Individual breakout strategies experience inevitable regime drawdowns. Portfolio
construction provides stability through the following core rules:

   The 100-to-10 Selection Ratio: To build a live portfolio of 10 to 15 breakout strategies, the research team
   generates and stress-tests at least 100 viable candidates, selecting only the least-correlated subset.

   Strict Correlation Gate: The average pairwise correlation across active portfolio strategies must remain
   strictly below 0.20 across rolling 12-month windows.

   Execution Cost & Slippage Modeling: All backtests deduct full exchange and brokerage commissions
   plus 1 to 2 ticks of adverse slippage on entry (for market and stop orders) and 1 to 2 ticks on exit.

   The "Zero-Stop" Stress Test: Before any strategy is approved, all stop-losses are deactivated. If the raw
   entry trigger and time exit cannot produce positive net profit after full transaction costs across at least 4 out
   of 5 related sector markets, the strategy is rejected as curve-fitted.
